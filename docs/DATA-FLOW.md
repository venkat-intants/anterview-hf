> Last updated: 2026-09-26 (see Change Log)
> This document is the authoritative record of every third-party sub-processor

> It is referenced from the in-app consent modal (all three Day-1 languages).

## Current Deployment Status

**IMPORTANT — India-Residency Status:**

The current ("Tier-1 demo") deployment is **NOT India-resident**. Data
processed during a candidate interview passes through sub-processors located
in **Singapore** (database), **United States** (LLM, avatar, voice), and
**global edge** (CDN/cache). This deployment is explicitly labelled a
**demo/early-access tier** and is **not suitable for government-bid
(APSSDC/NSDC) or DPDP-strict production use without migration to Tier-2**.

Tier-2 (AWS Mumbai, India-resident, DPDP-compliant production) is the target
for government contracts and is the same codebase with environment-level
configuration changes. See `Final_stack.md` for the Tier-2 stack and
`docs/PROCUREMENT.md` for the migration checklist.

This non-residency is recorded as an open, owned risk with a trigger for
revisiting — **[`ACCEPTED-RISKS.md` → AR-1](ACCEPTED-RISKS.md)**. The banner
above is the disclosure; AR-1 is the decision and who owns it.

Kept with the application as the company's assessment record until `TASK_SUBMISSION_RETENTION_DAYS` (180) after: a SUBMITTED task's application is decided (hired or rejected) — never while it is held or otherwise undecided, so an application abandoned without a decision keeps it until erasure; or an EXPIRED or WITHDRAWN submission simply closed, with no application decision to wait on — **only where `RETENTION_DRY_RUN=false`**; the default `true` counts and changes nothing. When the erasure then runs, any submission still open is withdrawn and its link killed; every response (text, link, title, description, file name and key) is redacted and the file deleted from storage (`erasure_executor` step 5i, step 8); the submission's status, timing and allowance snapshot are kept against the anonymised applicant, the exam-attempts precedent. HR's own task configuration and materials are company-authored and are not touched |

No free text. One live record per hire; a correction keeps the superseded row until retention or erasure removes the whole chain. HR sees each hire's own record to enter or correct it; analytics use it only in aggregate, with groups under 5 suppressed. Groups under 5 are suppressed in each response. This does not stop an HR manager, who can already read each hire's own record, from deriving a small group's figure by comparing responses. Never changes a status or decision, never read by the platform or across companies, never fed to any model. Because "employed" cannot be recorded before day 80, the DATE of a `checkin.recorded` audit row can itself imply the outcome, and that audit row outlives erasure (see AR-5). | Code quality and similarity evidence (PH4-D3) — static analysis of a coding-round submission (complexity and smells; the analyser never executes candidate code) and its winnowed fingerprint; when another submission, or the question's reference solution, overlaps enough to be worth a look, a SIGNAL names both attempts — automated and unreviewed, never a finding on its own. No HR screen receives the candidate's program output: the evidence tab and the attempt's score breakdown both reduce each test to pass/fail, with no stdout, stderr or hidden test cases (the breakdown did send them, unaudited, until a security review found it). A signal is the one place in this table where reviewing one candidate's coding round necessarily shows HR a SECOND, named candidate's code alongside it, whether or not that second candidate did anything wrong. A named HR manager's own judgement call on a signal or submission (an integrity FINDING, with a mandatory rationale) is the only one of these a person writes to | Neon (Singapore), so — like every other free-text field in this table — it leaves India (AR-1) | Kept with the application as the company's assessment record until `code_evidence_retention_days` (180) after the application is decided (or after submission, when there is no application) — **only where `RETENTION_DRY_RUN=false`**; the default `true` counts and changes nothing | On erasure the coding submission's SOURCE and its program's stdout/stderr are redacted from the attempt (the score is kept); the quality report, fingerprint and any similarity signal naming the erased attempt are deleted outright; an integrity finding's rationale is redacted too — on EITHER side of a signal's pair, since it can quote or name either candidate — while its outcome and who recorded it are kept (`erasure_executor` step 5h) |
| Document corpus (PH5-E2) — a company's OWN reference library the staff copilot can search: policies, handbooks, process notes, uploaded by its `hr_manager`/`super_admin` staff, never by a candidate. The original uploaded file: the same object store and bucket as CVs, preboarding documents and task artifacts — in the demo tier Cloudflare R2 (US), so it leaves India (AR-1). Embeddings are produced by Google Gemini (`gemini-embedding-001`, see the corrected sub-processor row above) — United States | Kept as the company's reference library for as long as HR keeps the document. A superseded version's TEXT (chunks, embeddings, the stored file) is purged `CORPUS_SUPERSEDED_RETENTION_DAYS` (180) after a newer version replaces it — the version ROW survives, so a citation naming it still resolves, to a "replaced, text removed" notice rather than a broken link. The controls that exist instead: the upload attestation, the audience default described above, and an IMMEDIATE (no grace window) purge of a document's chunks, embeddings AND its originally uploaded FILE in R2 — not only the searchable chunk copy — the moment HR deletes it |
| Rediscovery consent (PH5-E3) — a candidate's per-COMPANY, opt-in choice to be considered for future openings at that company, given from the public apply form (unauthenticated) or from their own "My applications" page (signed in). **The apply form records it from either of its two doors**: the single-shot submit, or a saved draft's later submission — `POST /apply/{id}/draft` stores the ticked box on the draft and `submit_draft` is what actually calls the same writer, so ticking the box and using "Save for later" no longer drops the choice silently (code review FIX 2, 2026-09-26; before this fix the draft path had nowhere to keep it). **No new table**: it is one more row shape in the existing DPDP consent ledger (`consent_type='talent_pool_rediscovery'`, `purpose='rediscovery'`), the same route PH4-D4 cut for per-submission task consent — the ledger is the record, so there is nothing else to keep in sync. An erasure REQUEST revokes every ledger row for the person at request time (`routers/erasure.py`, before the 30-day grace window), so a rediscovery search stops matching within seconds; the eligibility query also excludes anyone with an open `erasure_requests` row independently. **Purpose limitation, stated plainly:** this consent governs only whether a candidate can be FOUND by a rediscovery search and CONTACTED about a new opening — it does not govern whether HR can see a name it already holds under the application-data consent on `/hr/applicants/{id}`, so a pool entry a candidate is already in is not deleted by withdrawal or expiry, only marked not-currently-contactable (see the next row) |
| Talent pools and their membership (PH5-E3) — a company's OWN named shortlists of candidates it may want to contact about a FUTURE opening: the pool's name and description (HR's own words about the LIST, never about a person); each member's addition date, source (typed in by HR, or carried over from a rediscovery search result), HR's free-text note about that person, a FROZEN "why this matched" snapshot as at the time they were added (facts only — signal names, scores, competency ids, timestamps; no CV prose, no lexical snippet, re-stripped on the way into the database regardless of what a client sends), and whether a named person has since reviewed the evidence. A MEMBERSHIP row's usable life is governed by the rediscovery consent above, not by a separate clock: a member whose consent has lapsed or been withdrawn still lists, but renders not-eligible with no evidence panel and no invite action | On erasure, the candidate's membership row in EVERY pool at every company is HARD-DELETED (`erasure_executor` step 5k) — deliberately not kept-and-anonymised the way `enrolments`/`round_results` are: a pool is forward-looking, and a membership row has no backward-looking value as a hiring record once the person cannot be contacted again, and the note is prose HR wrote about them. The pool itself and its event trail are NOT touched by erasure (`EXCLUDED_TABLES`): a pool has no candidate column of its own, and the trail's facts-only design means it can keep saying "an applicant was added" after that applicant is anonymised without naming anybody — the same position `enrolments`/`round_results` are already in (see `ACCEPTED-RISKS.md` AR-5's extended scope note). ## Candidate Rights (DPDP Act 2023)

Under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023, candidates have:

- **Right of Access** — request a copy of your data: email support@intants.com
- **Right to Correction** — update your profile at any time via the dashboard
- **Right to Erasure** — request account + data deletion: email support@intants.com
  or use the in-dashboard delete option (when available)
- **Right to Withdraw Consent** — consent can be withdrawn at any time by
  emailing support@intants.com; withdrawal ends all active and future recording.
  Sessions already completed are retained for the stated retention period unless
  an erasure request is also submitted.
- **Right to Grievance Redressal** — complaints addressed within 30 days by the
  platform owner (support@intants.com)
